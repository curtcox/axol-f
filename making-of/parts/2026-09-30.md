#### Claude · 08:59

I'd start with **Spanish and Brazilian Portuguese**, then **Japanese**. After that, German, French and Hindi.

**Why those three first**
- **Spanish** and **Brazilian Portuguese** are the two largest YouTube audiences after English, and many of those viewers don't follow fast spoken English. Both use the Latin alphabet, so the words drawn in the picture (cards, captions, labels) fit without new fonts or layout changes. ElevenLabs dubbing is strong in both.
- **Japanese** has a big audience interested in AI, English there is weak, and viewers watch dubbed and subtitled videos. It costs more: the drawn text needs a Japanese font and the layout needs checking, because line lengths and line breaks change.

**Later**
- **German and French** have a strong interest in AI policy, but many of those viewers already manage English. Subtitles may be enough.
- **Hindi** is YouTube's biggest market, but Indian tech viewers mostly watch in English. Hindi subtitles are cheap to add; a dub is worth less.
- **Chinese, Korean and Arabic** are big but cost more. YouTube is blocked in mainland China, Korean needs another new script, and Arabic runs right to left, which the site would need to handle.

**Things that affect cost for any language**
- **Text in the picture.** Captions and cards are drawn into the frames, so a real translated film means a full render per language (about 5.5 hours each). The cheaper route is one English video with extra audio tracks: YouTube supports several dubbed tracks on one video. Viewers would still see English cards.
- **The references.** Most of the 408 codes point to English sources. The 64 explainers can be translated, but the pages they link to stay English. That matters less for German and French readers than for Brazilian or Japanese ones.
- **The voices.** Your cloned voice and Claude's voice can speak other languages through ElevenLabs. The lip sync is computed from each audio track, so it would carry over on its own. The name pronunciations would need checking again by ear for each language.
- **Cheapest first step:** translated YouTube subtitles in many languages, made from the script. Save dubs and translated sites for the two or three languages above.

One decision is yours before I plan any of this: fully translated films with the text in the picture re-rendered, or the English picture with dubbed audio and subtitles.
